What To Do If You Think an Email Is Suspicious

  1. Do not click suspicious links or open attachments
  2. Forward the email to the security team.
  3. Report anything clicked or any information entered.
  4. Delete the email after confirmation from our IT team.

How to Spot Phishing Emails

1. Check the Sender

Attackers often use email addresses that look similar to legitimate ones.

Example:

  • company-support.com
  • companysupp0rt.com
  • company-secure.net

In this case: myhealthhubclinic.com not healthhubclinic.com.

This happens to be an additional domain that we own, but attackers may try to spoof or even purchase a similar domain.

Always verify the full sender address.

2. Be Cautious of Urgency

Phishing emails often try to create panic:

  • "Your account will be suspended."
  • "Immediate action required."
  • "Payment overdue."
  • "Verify now."

In this case: “important Note regarding SOP”

Urgency is designed to make you act before you think.

3. Hover Over Links

Before clicking, hover over links to see where they actually go.

Ask yourself:

  • Does the destination match the company website?
  • Is it misspelled?
  • Does it use a strange domain?

In this case: “dreamstation.systems” not google.com

If unsure, visit the site directly through your browser instead.

4. Never Enter Credentials Through Email Links

Whenever possible:

  • Navigate directly to the website.
  • Use saved bookmarks.
  • Access services through approved company portals.

In this case: Enter your account email information

Avoid logging in through links received unexpectedly via email.

5. Verify Unusual Requests

For requests involving:

  • Passwords
  • MFA approval
  • Payments
  • Gift cards
  • Sensitive data

In this case: There was no unusual request, which makes it more difficult to determine. Unusual requests should be the final red flag to warrant a report to our IT team.

Contact the sender through a known, trusted method before taking action.
