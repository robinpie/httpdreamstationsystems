ubuntu804 — an Ubuntu 8.04 desktop as a /personal/ theme
========================================================

The fifth theme in the /personal/ switcher. Picking it wraps the page in a
picture of an Ubuntu 8.04 LTS "Hardy Heron" desktop running Firefox 3: GNOME
panel with a live clock, metacity window frame, a menu bar whose seven menus
really open, the navigation toolbar, the bookmarks toolbar, a status bar, and
the exposed wallpaper strip along the bottom with the badge shelf on it.

It is the same desktop as the standalone template under /tmp-ubuntu804/ — see
ubuntu804/ubuntu804template.txt for that one — but reached a different way, and
the difference is the whole point of this file.

  /tmp-ubuntu804/   build.pl generates complete pages around chrome.html.
                    Every hook the stylesheet wants is in the generated markup.
  this theme        the pages are hand-written, predate the theme by years, and
                    must still render under four other stylesheets. Nothing may
                    be generated and nothing may be permanently restructured.

WHERE EVERYTHING IS
-------------------
  rootdomain/personal/themes/ubuntu804.css      the stylesheet
  rootdomain/personal/themes/ubuntu804/i/       11 images (wallpaper, icons)
  rootdomain/personal/themes/ubuntu804/f/       two DejaVu woff2 faces
  rootdomain/personal/themes/ubuntu804/CREDITS.txt   attribution; linked from
                                                the theme's own Help menu
  rootdomain/personal/chrome/top.html           the desktop markup, in three
  rootdomain/personal/chrome/mid.html           unbalanced fragments
  rootdomain/personal/chrome/bottom.html
  nginx/snippets/theme.conf                     the internal chrome location
  nginx/sites-available/dreamstation.systems    ubuntu804 in both theme maps

The asset copies are byte-identical to what /tmp-ubuntu804/ ships and were
taken from the same sources build.pl's @ASSETS table uses. They are DUPLICATED
rather than shared on purpose: ubuntu804template.txt documents removing that
tree with a `git rm -r`, and a theme that dies when a temporary template is
deleted is a theme with a trapdoor under it. 464 kB, most of it the two fonts.

HOW IT WORKS
------------
The seven themed pages already run through nginx with `ssi on` — that is how
they pick a stylesheet at all. So the chrome rides the same machinery: each
page carries three include lines, each guarded on the theme, and under
gtk2/motif/skeuslop/plain nginx expands them to nothing. Measured: 5.5 kB for a
page under any other theme, 21.7 kB under this one.

The fragments are served from their own location — `internal`, and `ssi on`
again. Both are load-bearing; see THINGS THAT WILL BITE.

THREE FRAGMENTS, NOT TWO
------------------------
The obvious split is a prologue and an epilogue. It does not work, because one
piece of the desktop has to come from the page itself and land in the middle.

    [top]      .desktop > .panel, .window > .titlebar,
               .client > .menubar, .navbar            (.client left open)
    <header>   the page's own nav -> THE BOOKMARKS TOOLBAR
    [mid]      .viewport > #page > #masthead   (.viewport, #page left open)
    <main>     the page -> the document in the browser viewport
    <footer>   the page's own badges -> THE BADGE SHELF
    [bottom]   close #page/.viewport, .statusbar, close .client/.window,
               close .desktop

The site nav has to sit BETWEEN the navigation toolbar and the viewport,
because on a real Firefox window the bookmarks toolbar is chrome and does not
scroll, while the masthead is the top of the document and does. Those two are
on opposite sides of the scroll container's boundary, and no stylesheet can
move an element across it. Hence a fragment on each side of the header.

THREE ELEMENTS RE-CAST
----------------------
The pages needed almost nothing added because three things they already had
turned out to be the things the desktop wanted:

  header.site-header  ->  the bookmarks toolbar. Already a row of links, which
                          is what that toolbar is. `display: contents` on the
                          <nav> inside dissolves it so the links become flex
                          items of the bar directly, which is what lets a
                          two-element header (nav + theme switcher) lay out as
                          one toolbar with the switcher on the right end.
  main                ->  the document. Gains id="content" so the skip link has
                          somewhere to land; that is the only markup change to
                          the pages besides the includes and the new <option>.
  footer ul.badges    ->  the badge shelf on the exposed wallpaper. Both were
                          already 88x31 walls. They line up exactly.

The footer is the one that needs a trick. The shelf sits OUTSIDE the window,
but the footer's place in the markup is inside it, in the middle of the page,
where no include split can reach. So the theme gives it `position: fixed` and
pins it to the bottom strip, and .desktop is short by exactly that strip's
height to leave the gap. Fixed descendants are not clipped by an ancestor's
overflow, so the shelf stays put while the page scrolls behind it — which is
the behaviour wanted anyway.

The theme switcher needed no trick at all: base.css already pins it with
position:fixed, so it was never in the flow to begin with. This theme puts it
back (position:static) and lets it ride the right end of the bookmarks toolbar,
because under this theme the top-right corner of the viewport is the middle of
the GNOME panel.

WHAT IS PER-PAGE, AND HOW IT GETS THERE
---------------------------------------
Two things in the chrome differ per page: the window title and the location
bar. Both are handed in by the page, on the same line as the first include:

    set wtitle -> the page's own <title>, verbatim
    set path   -> $document_uri

and the fragment echoes them. It has to be this way round; see the first trap
below. #echo defaults to encoding=entity, so a value taken from a request
variable is HTML-escaped before it lands in the markup.

$document_uri is right even for /personal/ with no filename: the server-level
`index` makes that an internal redirect to /personal/index.html before the
variable is read. Verified, not assumed.

THINGS THAT WILL BITE
---------------------
  - NGINX VARIABLES DO NOT SURVIVE AN #include virtual. The include is a
    subrequest and they are re-evaluated against it, so inside a fragment
    $document_uri names THE FRAGMENT and $theme resolves to the map default
    rather than the visitor's theme. Variables set with #set do arrive, because
    SSI looks them up in the main request as well as the subrequest. This is
    why wtitle and path are set by the page and not read in the fragment, and
    it is silent when you get it wrong — the location bar just shows
    /personal/chrome/top.html and nothing errors.

  - SSI DOES NOT UNDERSTAND HTML COMMENT NESTING. It scans the byte stream for
    its own opening sequence, so an SSI directive written inside an HTML
    comment as documentation is not an example, it is executed. This bit once
    already: chrome/top.html's header comment illustrated the theme guard, and
    the illustration ran. Describe the syntax in these files; do not spell it.

  - `ssi on` IS NEEDED ON THE FRAGMENT LOCATION TOO, not just on the pages.
    Whether a subrequest's output is SSI-parsed is decided by the config of the
    location that serves IT. Without it the panel clock reaches the browser as
    a literal SSI comment and never renders, and nothing warns you.

  - MALFORMED MARKUP IN A PAGE NOW BREAKS THE WINDOW. Under a plain stylesheet
    an unclosed tag near the end of a page is cosmetic. Here everything after
    it gets nested inside it, including bottom.html's closing structure.
    index.html had exactly this — a footer <img> missing its ">" swallowed the
    </a>, and the status bar ended up inside a link to archlinux.org. Fixed.
    If the chrome ever comes out visibly wrong on one page and right on the
    others, parse that page before suspecting the theme.

  - THE VIEWPORT IS A NESTED SCROLLER. body has overflow:hidden and the
    document scrolls inside .viewport, which is deliberate and matches
    /tmp-ubuntu804/. The long posts (gzipt, ntppost, slowqotd) therefore scroll
    inside the window rather than moving the page. Correct, but it is a
    different feel from the other four themes and is the thing most likely to
    be reported as a bug.

  - THE FULL FONTS SHIP, NOT A SUBSET, and that is deliberate. A subset is
    declared with a unicode-range, and the browser consults the range BEFORE it
    looks inside the file: a codepoint in range but absent from the font falls
    out of the family rather than through to the next @font-face. These pages
    are full of emoji — the nav alone has four — and DejaVu has none of them at
    any weight, so a census-derived subset either excludes them (and every page
    drags the full face down anyway looking for them, buying nothing) or
    includes them (and the range lies, and the emoji stop rendering). Full
    faces cost one fetch, need no build step, and cannot rot when a page gains
    a character.

RELATIONSHIP TO ubuntu804/chrome.html
-------------------------------------
The two carry the same menus and the same window furniture and are maintained
by hand in parallel. They are NOT generated from a common source, and a look at
what actually differs says why that would have bought little: the menu
destinations differ (this one's View and Bookmarks menus mirror /personal/'s
nav, its Help menu points at this theme's own CREDITS.txt), the per-page values
arrive by a completely different mechanism, and this one is split in three
around markup the other does not have. What is genuinely shared is the artwork
and the measurements, and those are shared — the images are byte copies and the
stylesheet's chrome rules came over from ubuntu804/site.css with their
reasoning attached.

If the menus change in one, change them in the other.

CHANGING THE STRIP HEIGHT
-------------------------
--strip-h in ubuntu804.css, and the sentence in the footer's ::after that says
what it is. Two places, on purpose: the note is generated content, so it cannot
read the custom property. /tmp-ubuntu804/ has the same number in site.conf and
they do not have to agree — they are separate trees.

TESTING IT
----------
There is no browser on the server, so this was verified structurally: every
page parsed with html5lib to confirm the chrome nests as intended, every asset
URL fetched for a 200, every theme fetched to confirm the fragments expand to
nothing under the other four and that an unknown theme id falls back to gtk2.
The look itself has NOT been eyeballed on the server and cannot be. Do that on
a workstation. ubuntu804/devserver.sh is the model for a loopback nginx that
serves the repo without committing anything; the same trick works here by
pointing the root at rootdomain/ and including nginx/snippets/theme.conf plus
the two map blocks out of the vhost.
